A useful project video begins with a result that viewers can understand. Show the important outcome, explain the problem, and choose the evidence that actually supports your story.

Plan the sequence before adding effects. Give every shot a purpose. Use a diagram to explain an idea, and use genuine screen recordings to demonstrate what the application does.

Choose a layout for the audience. A landscape comparison can place two views beside each other. A portrait explanation can place the main demonstration above the supporting detail.

Use movement to direct attention. Reveal a label when it becomes relevant, hold a readable screen long enough to follow it, and let a transition connect related ideas.

Finish by checking the picture, captions, and sound. Keep the editable project and original sources, so a later correction can become a focused revision instead of a complete restart.
