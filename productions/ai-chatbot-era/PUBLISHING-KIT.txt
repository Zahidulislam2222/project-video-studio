Now the other side:

→ Air Canada's chatbot told a customer the wrong refund policy. A tribunal said the airline was responsible. It had to pay.
→ In 2025, Klarna's CEO said cutting too deep into human support lowered quality. They started hiring people again.

So the lesson isn't "replace your team with a bot."
It's this:

A chatbot is only as good as its design.

What a well-built one does for an app or website:
1️⃣ Answers customers 24/7, in their language
2️⃣ Tracks orders, finds products, checks stock, books appointments
3️⃣ Lets owners upload products just by describing them
4️⃣ Reads invoices and turns them into reports
5️⃣ Sends low-stock alerts, price updates and weekly summaries
6️⃣ Flags suspicious logins and keeps an audit log
7️⃣ Hands complex or emotional cases to a human, with full context

And the 5 guardrails that keep the error rate low:
✅ Answers grounded in your real data
✅ Limited permissions
✅ Confirmation before important actions
✅ Every action logged
✅ A human one click away

That's where the real savings come from. Not from removing people, but from not wasting them on the same question a thousand times.
